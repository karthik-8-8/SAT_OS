### Theory of Working of MQ-2 Gas Sensor

The **MQ-2 gas sensor** is a semiconductor-type gas sensor used to detect the presence and approximate concentration of combustible and reducing gases such as **LPG, propane, methane, hydrogen, smoke, and alcohol vapors**.

#### Working Principle

The MQ-2 sensor contains a sensing material made primarily of **tin dioxide (SnO₂)**. It also has a small **heater coil** that keeps the sensing material at a suitable operating temperature.

When clean air is present, oxygen molecules are adsorbed onto the surface of the SnO₂ material. These oxygen molecules capture electrons from the sensing material, which increases its electrical resistance.

When a combustible or reducing gas comes into contact with the heated SnO₂ surface, it reacts with the adsorbed oxygen. This releases electrons back into the sensing material, causing its **electrical resistance to decrease**.

The change in resistance is measured as a change in the sensor's output voltage. A microcontroller such as Arduino can read this voltage and determine whether the concentration of gas has increased or decreased.

The sensor is commonly connected as a **voltage divider**, where the sensing resistance (R_S) changes according to the gas concentration:

[
V_{OUT}=V_C\frac{R_L}{R_S+R_L}
]

where:

* (V_{OUT}) = sensor output voltage
* (V_C) = supply voltage
* (R_L) = load resistance
* (R_S) = resistance of the gas-sensitive material

As the concentration of combustible gas increases, (R_S) generally decreases, resulting in an increase in the output voltage.

### Working Sequence

**Gas enters the sensor → gas reacts with heated SnO₂ → sensor resistance changes → output voltage changes → microcontroller reads the voltage → gas presence/concentration is estimated.**

The MQ-2 is widely used in **gas leakage detection, smoke detection, air-quality monitoring, and safety systems**. It is important to note that the MQ-2 is best suited for **relative gas detection**, and accurate concentration measurements require proper calibration.










### Theory of Working of BMP280

The **BMP280** is a digital **barometric pressure and temperature sensor** developed by Bosch. It measures atmospheric pressure and temperature and can be used to determine **altitude** and changes in atmospheric conditions.

#### Working Principle

The BMP280 works mainly using a **MEMS piezoresistive pressure-sensing element**. When atmospheric pressure acts on the sensor's tiny diaphragm, the diaphragm undergoes a small deformation. This deformation changes the electrical resistance of piezoresistive elements on the diaphragm.

The sensor converts this change in resistance into an electrical signal. An internal **ADC (Analog-to-Digital Converter)** digitizes the signal, and the BMP280's internal processing circuitry applies calibration data stored in the sensor.

The BMP280 also contains a temperature-sensing element. The measured temperature is used to compensate for the temperature dependence of the pressure sensor, improving measurement accuracy.

The processed pressure and temperature values are made available digitally through **I²C or SPI communication**.

#### Pressure-to-Altitude Measurement

Atmospheric pressure decreases as altitude increases. Therefore, the BMP280 can estimate altitude from measured pressure using the barometric relationship:

[
h = 44330\left[1-\left(\frac{P}{P_0}\right)^{1/5.255}\right]
]

where:

* (h) = altitude in metres
* (P) = measured atmospheric pressure
* (P_0) = reference pressure at sea level

Thus, the BMP280 can be used in applications such as **altimeters, weather stations, drones, navigation systems, and indoor/outdoor monitoring systems**.

**In short:** Atmospheric pressure → MEMS diaphragm deformation → resistance change → ADC conversion → temperature compensation/calibration → digital pressure and temperature output.

### Theory of Working of MPU6050

The **MPU6050** is a **6-axis MEMS motion sensor** that combines a **3-axis accelerometer** and a **3-axis gyroscope** in a single device. It is commonly used in robotics, drones, wearable devices, balancing systems, and motion-detection applications.

#### Working Principle

The MPU6050 uses **MEMS (Micro-Electro-Mechanical Systems)** technology to detect motion.

**1. Accelerometer:**
The 3-axis accelerometer measures acceleration along the **X, Y, and Z axes**. Inside the sensor, tiny mechanical structures move when the sensor experiences acceleration or changes in orientation. This movement causes a change in capacitance, which is converted into a digital acceleration value.

The accelerometer can also detect the effect of **gravity**, allowing the orientation or tilt of the device to be estimated.

**2. Gyroscope:**
The 3-axis gyroscope measures the **angular velocity** or rate of rotation around the X, Y, and Z axes. It uses a vibrating MEMS structure based on the **Coriolis effect**. When the sensor rotates, the vibrating structure experiences a force that causes a measurable displacement. This displacement is converted into angular velocity.

**3. Digital Processing:**
The signals from the accelerometer and gyroscope are converted into digital values using internal ADCs. The MPU6050 provides these values to a microcontroller through the **I²C communication interface**.

The sensor also contains an internal **Digital Motion Processor (DMP)** that can perform motion-processing operations and help calculate orientation-related information.

### Working Sequence

**Motion/rotation occurs → MEMS structures move → capacitance changes → electrical signals are generated → signals are converted to digital values → data is transmitted through I²C → microcontroller processes the motion data.**

### Applications

The MPU6050 is commonly used in:

* Robotics and robot balancing
* Drone stabilization
* Motion and gesture detection
* Inertial measurement systems
* Wearable devices
* Vehicle and object orientation detection
* Gaming controllers

**In short:** The accelerometer measures **linear acceleration**, while the gyroscope measures **angular velocity**. By combining both measurements, the MPU6050 can provide information about the **motion and orientation** of an object.
